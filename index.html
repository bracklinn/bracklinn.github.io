Working Papers

Resilience and Fragility in a Risk-Centric Model: Monetary Policy as Market Selection

Jonathan Goldberg, David López-Salido, and Bracklinn Williams

Abstract

An economy can recover from a recession without becoming resilient to the next one. In a business cycle model in which investors disagree about crash and recovery risks, we show that monetary policy can build resilience by leaning against speculative booms. This policy shifts market selection across cycles toward high-valuation investors (optimists), whose wealth supports asset prices and aggregate demand during recessions. Optimists collect risk premia during booms, but recurrent crashes can erode their wealth and create a fragility trap with persistently severe demand recessions. Leaning can break the trap by preserving optimists' wealth through crashes. However, leaning and tighter leverage limits also slow optimists' rebuilding after busts. Excessively tight leverage limits can therefore create a trap in an economy that would otherwise become resilient. We characterize optimal monetary policy, which phases in leaning as booms unfold. Quantitatively, the benefits of preserving risk-bearing capacity across future cycles justify leaning even at low optimist wealth shares, where a single-cycle planner would not lean.
